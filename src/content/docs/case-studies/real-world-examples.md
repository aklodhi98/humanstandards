Theory is useful, but seeing how principles play out in real projects is essential. These examples show human factors work in practice — the context, the constraints, what was tried, and what happened.

## GOV.UK: Making government services usable

**Context:** UK government services were fragmented across hundreds of websites, each with different designs, confusing language, and poor accessibility.

**Intervention:** Created a unified design system with plain language guidelines, consistent patterns, and mandatory accessibility testing. All services moved to GOV.UK.

**Results:**
- 80% of users can complete tasks without help (up from ~30%)
- Dramatic reduction in support calls
- Became the model for government digital services worldwide

**Lessons:**
- Consistency across services reduces cognitive load
- Plain language isn't "dumbing down" — it's respecting users' time
- Centralized design systems scale expertise

## Estonian e-Residency: Digital identity for everyone

**Context:** Estonia wanted to offer digital identity to non-residents for business and banking. Users would have no local support and varied technical literacy.

**Intervention:** Designed onboarding for zero assumed knowledge. Extensive testing with users from different countries and backgrounds. Clear error recovery for every failure state.

**Results:**
- Over 100,000 e-residents from 170+ countries
- High completion rates despite complex identity verification
- Minimal support burden relative to user base

**Lessons:**
- International users reveal localization assumptions you didn't know you had
- Error messages are UI — invest in them accordingly
- Progressive disclosure works for complex processes

## Healthcare.gov: Recovery from disaster

**Context:** Initial launch in 2013 was a public failure — site couldn't handle load, registration failed constantly, accessibility was poor.

**Intervention:** Brought in new team with agile practices. Rebuilt with performance budgets, extensive error handling, and systematic accessibility audits.

**Results:**
- From <1% registration completion to >90%
- Handled 100x the original traffic
- Became a model for government tech rescue

**Lessons:**
- Technical performance is UX — slow and broken isn't usable
- Never launch without load testing at expected scale
- Small, focused teams can move faster than large ones

## Banking apps: Authentication flow redesign

**Context:** A major bank saw high abandonment during mobile app registration. Users failed authentication and didn't retry.

**Intervention:**
- Simplified password requirements (removed arbitrary rules)
- Added real-time password strength feedback
- Reduced steps from 7 to 4
- Provided clear recovery paths for every error

**Results:**
- Registration completion increased 35%
- Support calls for login issues dropped 40%
- No increase in fraud or security incidents

**Lessons:**
- Complex password rules don't improve security — they just frustrate users
- Every step you remove is friction eliminated
- "Secure" and "usable" aren't opposites

## Writing your own case studies

Use this structure:

1. **Context**: What was the situation? Who were the users? What were the constraints?
2. **Problem**: What specifically wasn't working? How did you know?
3. **Intervention**: What did you change? Why did you believe it would help?
4. **Results**: What happened? Include numbers where possible.
5. **Lessons**: What's transferable? What would you do differently?
