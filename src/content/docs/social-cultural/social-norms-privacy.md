---
title: Social Norms & Privacy
description: Respect norms and minimize data; make consent informed and revocable.
---

Privacy isn't just a legal checkbox — it's a fundamental aspect of user trust. How you collect, store, and share data shapes how users feel about your product. Respect their expectations, be transparent, and give them control.

## Data minimization

### Collect only what you need

Every data point you collect is:
- A responsibility to protect
- A potential liability if breached
- A decision users have to make about trusting you

Ask yourself: do we actually need this information? Can we provide the same value without it?

### Minimize retention

Data you don't keep can't be breached. Delete data when it's no longer needed. Anonymize when possible. Set retention policies and enforce them.

### Least privilege

Limit access to personal data within your organization. Not everyone needs to see user emails, phone numbers, or addresses. Audit who has access regularly.

## Informed consent

### Make consent meaningful

Dark patterns in consent are both unethical and increasingly illegal:

- **Don't pre-check boxes** for marketing or data sharing
- **Don't hide opt-outs** in walls of legal text
- **Don't make rejection harder** than acceptance
- **Do use plain language** to explain what you're asking for and why

### Be specific

"We use your data to improve our services" tells users nothing. Instead:
- "We use your location to show nearby results"
- "We share your email with shipping partners to send tracking updates"
- "We analyze usage patterns to fix bugs (no personal data leaves your device)"

### Make it revocable

Consent given should be consent easily withdrawn:
- Accessible privacy settings (not buried in submenus)
- Clear instructions for data deletion
- Prompt honoring of deletion requests

## Sharing scope

### Default to private

When users create content or profiles:
- Default visibility to private or limited
- Clearly show who can see what before publishing
- Warn before actions that increase visibility

### Explain the audience

"Share" is vague. Tell users exactly who will see their content:
- "Share with 3 people in this workspace"
- "Post publicly (anyone on the internet)"
- "Visible to your 847 connections"

### Respect contextual integrity

People share different things in different contexts. Information shared with a doctor shouldn't appear in search results. Photos shared with close friends shouldn't show up in recommendations to strangers. Preserve the context in which data was shared.

## Social features

### Activity visibility

Before showing "Sarah is reading your message" or "John is online now", ask whether users want that visibility. Many don't.

### Social proof and exposure

Showing "1,247 people are viewing this" creates pressure. Displaying who liked or viewed something can embarrass users. Let people control their visibility.

### Defaults matter enormously

Most users never change settings. Your defaults are your true values. If you default to maximum data sharing and then offer opt-outs, you've already made the choice for most users.

## Legal compliance

Regulations vary by jurisdiction but share common themes:

- **GDPR (EU)**: Strong consent requirements, right to deletion, data portability
- **CCPA/CPRA (California)**: Right to know, right to delete, opt-out of sale
- **LGPD (Brazil)**: Similar to GDPR, with local enforcement

Design for the strictest requirements and you'll likely comply everywhere.
